- **Nothing has been generated yet.** Every character sheet, clip and voice line below is a written prompt.
- **Copy whole blocks.** Each code block is one complete, self-contained prompt.
- **Pictures and words are kept apart.** The clip prompts in section 5 contain no narration, dialogue or on-screen text. Narration is in section 6 and subtitles are in section 7.
- **Characters are repeated word for word.** The style and character paragraphs are pasted in full into every prompt, so the look stays the same from clip to clip.

## 1. Research and story

**The place.** Aneyoshi is a hamlet of about 11 households on a hillside above a small bay near Miyako, Iwate Prefecture, on Japan's northeast coast.

**The stone.** After the 1933 tsunami, the survivors set up a stone tablet on the slope. Its inscription is usually translated as: "High dwellings are the peace and harmony of our descendants. Remember the calamity of the great tsunamis. Do not build any homes below this point."

**The two disasters.** The 1896 Meiji Sanriku tsunami and the 1933 Shōwa Sanriku tsunami both devastated the village. The New York Times reported in 2011 that only two villagers survived in 1896 and only four in 1933.

**What happened next.** The villagers kept their homes above the stone for nearly 80 years, even though the walk down to the harbour was long. Along the coast, many larger towns built on low ground behind sea walls.

**11 March 2011.** A magnitude 9.0 earthquake struck off the coast, and the tsunami that followed overtopped sea walls along the coast. At Aneyoshi the water ran up the valley and stopped about 300 feet (about 90 m) below the stone. The houses above it were untouched. The village leader, Tamishige Kimura, told the New York Times that the ancestors knew the horrors of tsunamis and set up the stone to warn them.

| Point | What sources say | How this video handles it |
|---|---|---|

| Size of the stone | Reports range from about 4 ft to about 10 ft | Shown as a "tall slab"; no size spoken |
| Exact inscription | Translations vary slightly | Voiceover reads a short paraphrase |
| Survivor counts | 2 (1896) and 4 (1933), from the 2011 NYT report | Spoken as reported |
| Distance below the stone | About 300 ft / 90 m in all reports | "About 300 feet" |
| Length of the shaking | Several minutes, depending on location | "For minutes" |
| Outsiders doubting the village | A storytelling device, not a documented quote | Voiced by the narrator, not by a named person |

## 6. Voiceover package

**Voice:** a warm, calm male storyteller with a touch of suspense, like a friend telling you something amazing. Not a news anchor. Speak slower and quieter in clip 9 and leave a beat of silence before the last sentence.

**Pace:** about 3 words a second. Total about 230 words, about 85 seconds of speech.

**Pronunciation:** say 1896 as "eighteen ninety-six", 1933 as "nineteen thirty-three" and 2011 as "twenty eleven".

**Engine:** OmniRoute/TTS in OMS Studio, or any voice tool. Export one file per clip as `TS_VO_01.mp3` to `TS_VO_09.mp3`.
